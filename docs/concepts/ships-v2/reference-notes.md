# Ships v2: reference notes (from Andy's images)

Kept so the images do not need re-reading. Plain notes on what Andy likes; the designs come only from these.

## Batch 1 (2026-11-27): Andy: "I love these ships' form factors and proportions, how their shape flows."

Four blueprint sheets: Lakon Type-6 (cargo variant), Saud Kruger Dolphin (passenger), Saud Kruger Orca (deep-space
explorer), Lakon Type-6 (explorer variant).

- **Lakon Type-6 (48 m long, 28 m wide, 15 m high):**
  - a compact, chunky, faceted box hull that is wide and flat-ish (about 1.7 x 1 x 0.55);
  - a shield-shaped front with a recessed central cockpit, flanked by angled side cheeks that hold small pods;
  - the top view is a coffin or shield outline: narrower nose, widest at the middle, twin engine pods at the back
    either side of a centre spine;
  - heavy panel lines, many chamfers, vents (orange grille blocks), round sensor discs, a ventral boarding ramp and a
    cargo hatch underneath;
  - a utilitarian, workhorse look. The same hull serves cargo and explorer, so variants differ by fittings, not shape.
- **Saud Kruger Dolphin (52 x 21 x 9 m):**
  - a long, sleek teardrop body, almost a smooth tube, with a huge dark glass canopy running along the whole top;
  - very low and long (length about 6 x height);
  - small swept delta wings at mid-body and swept tail fins at the back, plus small vertical fin tips;
  - a rounded nose and a pointed tail with a spike;
  - white hull with orange accent panels near the stern, smooth flowing curves and few hard edges: elegant and luxury.
- **Saud Kruger Orca (130 x 51 x 23 m):**
  - the same family as the Dolphin but much longer: a long, narrow, smooth hull with a very long dark glass top
    stripe, from a rounded nose tapering back;
  - multiple swept fin pairs along the sides, a flat underside and a boat-like cross-section (rounded top, flat
    bottom, side "cheeks");
  - many decks inside, which reads as a big, comfortable long-haul ship;
  - elegant, streamlined, big but graceful.
- **Common to all:**
  - believable, functional spaceships with landing gear, a flat-ish underside, readable silhouettes, and the
    proportions doing the talking;
  - shapes flow from nose to tail: the bow narrows, the mid-body is widest, the stern ends in clear engine blocks.
- **Colours seen:** mostly white or light grey hulls, dark glass, small orange accents (Dolphin), blue blueprint
  styling (only the sheet style, not the ship).

## Batch 2 (2026-11-27): Andy: "reference these geometries for the top view of different types of ships; many nice elements."

Two sheets of top-view silhouette studies (Star Racer, Niko Stupelo), 15 numbered shapes each, light grey hull with an
orange underlayer showing at the edges and stern.

- **Sheet 1:**
  - **Needle and dart shapes** (1, 2, 3, 9, 14, 15): long pointed or rounded noses, widening to a compact orange
    engine cluster at the back.
  - **Teardrop and shield shapes** (7, 10, 14, 15): one smooth outline, the nose rounded or pointed.
  - **Wide flying-wing and boomerang shapes** (4, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13): a central body with long swept wings, the wing
    edges curved like a manta.
  - **Arrowheads** (5, 7): wide triangular plans.
- **Sheet 2:** stranger and more varied plans:
  - **Bodies with booms or "legs"** (1, 3, 4, 14): a slim fuselage with angled side arms, or long trailing struts.
  - **Twin-boom and U shapes** (6, 7, 13): two long arms with a pod between.
  - **Boxy "H" shapes** (5, 12, 15): a central body between two flat side slabs or pods.
  - **Swept deltas and chevrons** (8, 9, 10).
  - **Rounded capsule** (11) and **teardrop with swept legs** (2).
- **Recurring elements:**
  - two-tone hulls: light grey top surfaces, with a warm orange lower layer that frames the edges and stern;
  - thin orange racing stripes that follow the curves;
  - a dark cockpit set into the centre spine, usually far back;
  - engines grouped in a tight cluster at the stern;
  - small fin or antenna spikes at the wing tips;
  - clean, smooth, flowing outlines with few greebles (small surface details).
- **Use:** a library of top-view outlines to give each ship type its own plan shape (Andy's words). Keep colours as a
  possibility; confirm with Andy before using the grey and orange scheme.

## Batch 3 (2026-11-27): Andy: "nice passenger super long haul ideas, shapes and proportions."

Four images, for the heavy (super long haul) passenger ship.

- **Sleek grey liner (line art on a grid):**
  - a very long, pointed, knife-like hull with a raised spine;
  - swept fins forward, and two big round engine discs (ring-shaped nacelles) on swept pylons at the rear;
  - dark accent bands and panels along the hull;
  - dynamic and fast-looking, with the length dominating.
- **Smooth white capsule liner (3D render):**
  - one huge smooth elongated pebble or egg hull with a rounded nose and a rounded tail;
  - engine nacelles tucked into scooped-out recesses in the hull's flanks, with a blue engine glow;
  - a dark stepped "lightning" groove running along the side;
  - grid-panelled windows or solar panels on the top, rows of small lit windows along the sides;
  - very clean and minimal.
- **White flat-bottomed liner with landing gear (3D render):**
  - a wide, low hull that rises like a pyramid or wedge to a small bridge tower near the back, with a round dome and antenna on top;
  - a long ribbed window band along the side and three rounded hatches;
  - a red pinstripe;
  - long side sponsons running back to box engines with a vertical tail fin;
  - visible landing legs and teal hover thrusters underneath;
  - a mixture of yacht and spaceship.
- **"L600 interplanetary space liner" (side profile):**
  - a long white airliner-like body: a pointed, slightly droopy nose and two rows of small round windows;
  - a big rounded belly bulge or keel underneath, with three hatches;
  - two engine nacelles at the rear, one high on a swept pylon and one low on the side;
  - bold red diagonal livery stripes and a red cheatline (the long stripe along the body);
  - the outline seen from above is a long fuselage with a small cross-shaped tail.
- **Common thread for big liners:**
  - very long and smooth;
  - white or light hulls with one bold accent colour (red or dark);
  - rows of small windows;
  - engines in separate rear nacelles on pylons;
  - a calm, luxurious, airliner or cruise feel.
